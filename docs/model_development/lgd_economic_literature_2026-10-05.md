# Economic loss and CMBS servicing data — literature review

October 5, 2026. Research review of primary papers and author/analyst reports. Scope: the CMBS trustee/servicer reporting family used in the new pilot, with Fannie multifamily distinguished. No model change or rate selection. This search did not establish a study that reconstructs a complete discounted cash ledger from our exact SEC exhibit or our current Fannie multifamily file.

## Findings

**Wong (2018), CMBS and Conflicts of Interest: Evidence from Ownership Changes for Servicers.** [Published article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/jofi.12690); [2016 author working paper](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2016/SI2016/REAL/Wong.pdf) inspected for definitions. Working version uses purchased Realpoint/Morningstar loan data; outcome is realized loss divided by pre-loss loan balance. Published abstract reports an eight-percentage-point increase in loss rates following selected servicer ownership changes, relative to comparison servicers. Fee/service-provider conflicts are a mechanism, not a reason to omit fees. Working version considers faster liquidation versus lower sale prices as a separate timing tradeoff, not a reconstructed discounted-LGD outcome. This is the same market/reporting family, not the identical public dataset; published and working-version specifications must not be conflated.

**D'Lima and Lopez (2021), Trustee affiliation and servicer oversight: Evidence from CMBS markets.** [Published article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1540-6229.12315); [2019 working version](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2019/preliminary/paper/dRfYQH2h) inspected. Working version uses Trepp and liquidation loss relative to balance one month before liquidation. Published abstract links affiliation to excessive P&I advances, changed tranche cash flows and five/seven cents greater loss per dollar of outstanding debt. Its economic-impact language denotes incremental liquidation losses, not necessarily discounted economic workout LGD. Advances can matter to bondholder cash allocation; a whole-loan loss view must separately reconcile them. Use published effect sizes rather than earlier working-version estimates.

**Gaon / Academy Securities (2023), Recovering the Non-Recoverable: Liquidation Waterfall Nuance Bolsters Bond Paydowns.** [Original report](https://academysecurities.com/wp-content/uploads/CMBS_Credit_Focus_NRA_Recovery-1.pdf), November 6. Uses investor remittance reporting/MCIA with analyst reconstruction. Some amounts recorded in liquidation expenses can correspond to recovered previously reimbursed non-recoverable advances and increase principal distributions. Expense breakdowns are often incomplete; timing between reimbursement and recovery varies. This directly supports reconciling advance movements and subsequent adjustments before labeling economic loss. It is practitioner research, not a peer-reviewed discounted-LGD estimation study or evidence about every payment in our pilot case.

**FDIC (2015), Loss Given Default for Commercial Loans at Failed Banks.** [Primary paper](https://archive.fdic.gov/view/fdic/11956/fdic_11956_DS1.pdf). Different administrative dataset. Missing full recovery histories are handled by inferring net principal recovery and assuming receipt at asset extinction; loan interest rate is used for discounting. Expenses are included but servicing costs omitted, which authors acknowledge limits the economic-loss definition. Higher discount-rate, carrying-cost and interest treatments are sensitivity alternatives. This establishes a defensible precedent for an explicitly assumption-based proxy when cash data are incomplete, not proof that the same reconstruction is identified for Fannie or CMBS accounting totals. We would not adopt its floors/caps or accounting assumptions automatically.

## Interpretation and recommendation

Fees and genuine expenses should not be excluded categorically. Their effect can be material, and servicing incentives can affect recoveries. Combined reported deductions still need decomposition because advances, reimbursements, holdbacks and fees can have different cash consequences. The literature reinforces defining the loss perspective and separating nominal liquidation severity from discounted workout LGD.

A useful next research design has three separate outputs: disclosed liquidation-loss severity; a reconciled cash/advance-adjustment view; and discounted proxy sensitivities under explicit default/EAD, recovery timing and rate assumptions. None should be relabeled a complete observed economic ledger. Do not mark assumptions as observed in the strict cash-flow interface. Link each assumption to evidence and test the sensitivity; uncertainty about missing components persists even when a formula is calculable.

For our existing Fannie multifamily data, a public predictive-model paper was discovered (The Advantage of Case-Tailored Information Metrics for the Development of Predictive Models, Calculated Profit in Credit Scoring; [publisher](https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/24/9/1218)), but full text retrieval failed in this search. No verified claim about its discounting/cost method is made. Public-data usage alone does not establish economic-LGD labels.

The strongest finding is therefore methodological: economic-loss approximations are possible with incomplete histories if assumptions are explicit. The search does not justify treating our combined deductions as complete dated costs, selecting a universal discount rate, or claiming improved prediction accuracy. CPI signed-source baseline, raw data and population remain unchanged. No contact with authors/providers or purchase/licensing was performed.
